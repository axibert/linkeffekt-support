1 · What is LinkEffekt?

LinkEffekt streams audio between music apps and devices, over your local network (via Ableton Link Audio) or over the internet (Online mode). It ships as three AUv3 plugins that run inside any AUv3 host (AUM, Logic Pro, Cubasis, Drambo, GarageBand and others):

PluginTypeUse it to
LinkEffekt SendAudio effectStream a track's audio out. Insert it on the track you want to share.
LinkEffekt ReceiveAudio effectReceive channels. Works in every host; this is the standard receiver.
LinkEffekt Receive InstrumentInstrumentThe same receiver as a sound source, for hosts that need one on the channel (e.g. as the source in AUM) or for Logic multi-output routing.

What can you do with it?

  • Jam with two devices in one room: each player’s iPad/iPhone streams its tracks to the others over Wi-Fi, beat-locked via Ableton Link (§2, §7).
  • Route audio between apps on one device: e.g. send a track from AUM into Cubasis to record it. No Wi-Fi, no setup, lowest latency (§2, “Same device or same host”).
  • Bring a remote friend into the session: stream over the internet, peer-to-peer and encrypted; their audio locks onto your bars (§3, §6).
  • Record iOS audio in Ableton Live: Live 12.4+ receives LinkEffekt Send directly as a Link Audio input (§7, FAQ).
  • Monitor a whole band on one device: several Send channels, summed in one Receive, or one Receive per channel for separate faders (§5).

Free vs. Unlimited Channels: the free version includes 1 Send and 1 Receive channel, fully functional on the local network. The one-time in-app purchase Unlimited Channels unlocks unlimited instances, monitoring multiple channels at once, and the Online (internet) mode. Purchase or restore it in the LinkEffekt app or directly inside the plugins.

First step on iOS: open the LinkEffekt app once after installing. This triggers the Local Network permission. Without it, the plugins cannot see each other.

2 · Quick start: local network (LAN)

Send and Receive work on two devices, in two apps on one device, and in the same host app on different tracks.

  1. Sender: insert LinkEffekt Send on a track. For other devices, tap the gear and enable Ableton Link and Share Audio. The orange banner disappears when sharing is active.
  2. Receiver: load LinkEffekt Receive on a track in the other app or on the other device (same Wi-Fi network).
  3. In the receiver, open the Now Receiving menu and pick the sender's channel.
  4. You should hear audio within a second and see the meter move. The dot next to the channel name turns green, the sender switches to “Streaming to a listener”, and the receiver's status line reads:
In sync

While there is no shared beat reference it reads “Free-run: playing at lowest latency” instead.

Tip: name your Send channels (the Name field). That name is what receivers see, and each additional Send instance appears as its own channel.

Same device or same host: app-to-app routing

Everything on one iPad/Mac? LinkEffekt routes these cases over a direct fast path that needs no Wi-Fi, no Local Network permission and no Ableton Link setup. It is the lowest-latency, most stable option:

  • A sender in another app on the same device appears as a channel tagged This device.
  • A sender inside the same host app (e.g. AUM track 1 to track 2) appears as This host.

Example, record an AUM setup into Cubasis: insert LinkEffekt Send on the AUM channel you want to capture, open Cubasis, insert LinkEffekt Receive on an audio track, pick the channel tagged This device, arm the track and record. The same recipe routes a synth app through another host’s effects, or feeds a DJ app’s output into a recorder: anywhere iOS offers no direct audio path between apps.

Latency on one device: most of the end-to-end latency comes from the two host apps' audio buffer sizes, not from LinkEffekt (its own share on a direct path is a few ms). For the tightest timing set both apps to 128 or 256 frames (AUM: Settings > Buffer Size; Cubasis: Setup > Audio > Latency). With Show diagnostics (gear) the Receive plugin shows the measured breakdown under the Latency slider: total, LinkEffekt's share, and the host apps' share.

3 · Quick start: Online (internet)

Online mode streams over the internet, peer-to-peer, encrypted end-to-end (DTLS). It requires Unlimited Channels on both sides.

  1. Person A: in LinkEffekt Send, switch Destination to Online, tap Create Room and share the room code (copy button next to the code).
  2. Person B: in LinkEffekt Receive, switch the top selector to Online, enter the code and tap Join.
  3. B picks A's channel from Now Receiving. For two-way audio, B also loads a Send (or uses Send my track, see §6).
  4. Press play on your host transport on both sides and set both hosts to the same tempo. The peer's audio locks onto your beat grid, delayed by whole bars (see §7).

The header shows an ONLINE badge with the room code whenever an instance is in Online mode: green dot = peer connected, orange = waiting for a peer.

4 · LinkEffekt Send: reference

  • Status line under the header: “Streaming to a listener” or “Waiting for a receiver”. Listeners on this device (This device / This host) count too. In Online mode the ONLINE badge in the header shows the room status instead.
  • Destination: LAN & Local / Online: where this track streams (LAN & Local always includes listeners on this device). Switching Online to LAN & Local removes only this track from the room; the room itself stays open until you tap Leave (a “room is still open” line with a Leave button stays visible in the LAN & Local tab).
  • Name: the channel name receivers see. Renames propagate live, also in Online rooms, and receivers keep their subscription.
  • Send Audio: master switch for streaming this track. Off = the plugin passes audio through locally but sends nothing.
  • Mute Local Output (default on): mutes the track locally after streaming, so you don't hear the signal twice (once dry, once through the receiver with latency). Turn it off only if the receiver is somewhere you can't hear.
  • Gain / Pan: applied to the stream (and to the local output), with click-free ramps. The bottom of the gain range is true silence.
  • Level meter: post gain/pan; red = clipping (tap to reset).
  • Live Mode (at the bottom, only in hosts with delay compensation such as AUM): LinkEffekt Send asks the host to render this track 50 ms ahead, so receivers get the audio before its beat and play it on their grid (§7). The host keeps its own tracks in time by delaying everything else in this app by the same 50 ms. When you play live in this app, those 50 ms are in your ears: turn Live Mode on. Receivers then play the stream with their normal latency. Receivers that are already listening follow by themselves: after switching it on, with a gap of about two seconds; after switching it off, with a short jump back onto the grid. The switch appears a few seconds after the host starts rendering, and only where the host really renders ahead (never in Cubasis, which has no delay compensation).
  • Gear (Ableton Link settings): the Link session and Share Audio (required for sending to other devices; Online and listeners on this device work without it). Below that:
    • Link Offset (±100 ms): shifts this channel's timing at every receiver. Negative offsets can cause dropouts on the receiver; raise the receiver's Latency if needed. Apply to all channels uses one value for all Send instances in this host.
    • Send at 48 kHz: for receivers that need 48 kHz, e.g. Ableton Live's Link input.
    • Show diagnostics: a technical readout for support requests, plus the plugin version.

Orange banner “Share Audio is off”: shown only where the switch actually blocks something, i.e. when nobody on this device is listening and the destination is LAN & Local. Tap it to open the settings.

Freemium: the second and further Send instances without Unlimited Channels keep passing audio through locally but do not stream (“Channel Limited” banner with an unlock button).

5 · LinkEffekt Receive: reference

  • LAN & Local / Online selector: which world the channel list below shows. LAN & Local covers network channels and the direct paths on this device (This host / This device). The header shows Link peers (LAN) or the room status (ONLINE badge).
  • Now Receiving: pick exactly one channel. “Channel (unavailable)” means the sender disappeared (app closed, network lost).
  • Monitor multiple channels (Unlimited Channels): sum several channels into this plugin's stereo output. Channels that are active but belong to the other transport tab are shown with a LAN/Local/Online tag so nothing is summed invisibly. For separate faders per channel, use one Receive instance per channel instead.
  • Sync status line (metronome icon): “In sync” = beat-locked and audio flowing; “In sync · locked to the bar” = Bar Sync placement (whole bars, see §7); “Buffering…” = normal for a moment after selecting a channel; “Free-run: playing at lowest latency” = there is no shared beat reference (audio flows normally, just not beat-aligned); “Playing free-run … enable Ableton Link (gear icon)” tells you how to get beat-locked on LAN; “Waiting for your host transport. Press play” (Online) = the beat lock is waiting for your host’s transport to run.
  • Bar Sync (LAN & Local tab; Online is always bar-synced): locks the incoming downbeat to your bar: one bar late, but rock-solid even over a shaky connection. Off = lowest latency. It is not offered on a direct connection (This device / This host), where the low-latency path is already tight, and it disappears when the audio already arrives on your grid (sender in AUM with Live Mode off, §7). With Bar Sync on, LinkEffekt also aligns the beat grids automatically: a steady offset between your host's grid and the shared Link clock is measured and corrected, with no control and no setup; where it cannot be measured, nothing changes.
  • Latency (3 to 200 ms, always visible in low-latency mode): a fixed value. By default LinkEffekt sets it once when you pick a channel: what this connection needs at minimum plus a reserve (25 ms over a network, 2 ms on the same device). The number appears half a second after you pick the channel. After that it does not move: a gap in the network longer than the reserve is a short dropout, and the audio returns on the beat. Move the slider to set your own value; it applies exactly and at once. Lower is tighter, higher rides out more; below what the connection physically needs, LinkEffekt uses that minimum instead (the label then says so). Default takes you back. If dropouts keep coming (more than two in ten minutes), their count appears under the slider: raise the value until it stays quiet.
    • If the connection itself changes while you play, LinkEffekt sets the value once more. Later audio (the sender switches Live Mode on, a device switches to Bluetooth headphones): when more than half of it is missing for a second while packets keep coming, the value goes up; you hear a gap of about two seconds. Earlier audio (the sender switches Live Mode off): when the connection needs more than a full reserve less for a second, the value goes down and the stream jumps forward onto the grid, without a dropout. Ordinary network hiccups and small changes never trigger this.
  • Advanced timing (only with Bar Sync and in Online mode, collapsed by default):
    • Nudge (±100 ms): fine-tunes where the received downbeat lands, minus = earlier, plus = later. A full bar of buffered audio gives room to play earlier or later. A non-zero value is shown next to the “Advanced timing” label. Apply to all channels in this host passes the same value to every LinkEffekt instance in that host, because what it corrects (the host's own output delay) belongs to the host, not to one channel.
  • Mixer block, in signal order: Received (mute the incoming stream), Host track (Receive effect only; the track's own input, default muted to prevent feedback, see §8), and Output with master gain/pan/mute.
  • Diagnostics line: appears under the channel menu only when something is wrong, in plain language (see §8 for what each message means). When audio is flowing there is no message: the green dot, the status line and the meter say it.
  • Gear, then Settings: Ableton Link settings plus Show diagnostics (a technical sync readout under the channel list, the measured latency breakdown and packet statistics; useful when reporting an issue, off by default).

Receive vs. Receive Instrument: identical engine. Use the effect everywhere you can insert it; use the Instrument where the channel needs a source (AUM source slot, Logic software-instrument track with multi-output).

6 · Online mode in detail

  • One room per host app. All Online instances inside the same host share one room and one connection. The first instance creates/joins; the others follow automatically. Leave disconnects all of the host's online tracks (the dialog says so).
  • Multiple tracks: every Send-Online instance is its own stream. The peer sees each one as a separate channel (up to 8 per direction).
  • Send my track (Receive effect in Online mode): also streams this track's input into the room, giving full two-way audio in a single plugin slot. Built for hosts with only one usable slot, e.g. DJ apps.
  • Room codes are one-time invitations relayed by the signaling server; the audio itself flows peer-to-peer, DTLS-encrypted. No audio passes through our servers. Rooms with only one participant close after 30 minutes; active sessions have no time limit.
  • Reconnect: if one side drops out, rejoin with the same code while the other side is still in the room. If both left, create a fresh room.

7 · Beat Sync: how it works and what to expect

Audio that travels through a network always arrives late; no software can remove that. What LinkEffekt can do is make the delay musical:

  • LAN (low latency, Bar Sync off): the receiver places the incoming audio onto your local Ableton Link grid. The result is a stable, grid-locked offset equal to the Latency value, instead of drifting timing. Both apps must be in the same Link session (Link enabled on both).
  • On the grid, with no offset: in a host with delay compensation (AUM), LinkEffekt Send has the host render its track 50 ms ahead. The audio then reaches the receiver before its beat, and the receiver plays it right on your grid at close to zero latency. Bar Sync is not needed then and disappears. The price is on the sender's side: everything in that app sounds 50 ms later. For material you play live there, turn Live Mode on in the Send (§4); the receiver then plays it with its normal latency.
  • Bar Sync (LAN) / Online: the delay is rounded up to whole bars on your host's beat grid. The incoming “1” lands exactly on your “1”, typically one bar later (2 s at 120 BPM). Online requirements: host transport playing on both sides, same tempo on both hosts. When you press play, expect about one bar of silence while the buffer fills, then it locks. The constant difference between your grid and the Link clock is measured and removed automatically where it is measurable; a remaining constant offset (for example a live-played source's own input chain) is what Nudge is for.
  • Free-run is what you get without a shared beat reference: lowest latency, no alignment. Fine for monitoring, speech, or when you play by ear.
  • A constant offset in low-latency mode, when the sender does not render ahead: received audio sits as far behind your local tracks as the Latency value, plus whatever your host delays after the plugin (Cubasis, for example, does not compensate plugin latency at all). LinkEffekt Receive deliberately does not report its latency to the host: a host that compensates answers by moving the plugin's own clock, and the buffer is measured against exactly that clock. So the offset is by design, and it is the normal situation for any live signal. It does not matter for monitoring; for grid-accurate work turn on Bar Sync (exact placement, one bar late) and use Nudge for the last few milliseconds.
  • Cubasis: turn off Multi-core Processing (Setup, Audio). That setting is the extra buffer Cubasis puts behind the plugin without reporting it; Cubasis shows it as Guard buffer. With it off, the guard buffer is 0 ms and the received audio is tight without any Nudge. The audio buffer size then no longer affects timing accuracy, so pick one that keeps the engine comfortable. If you must keep Multi-core on, read the Guard buffer value (Setup, Audio, tap the latency total) and set Nudge to minus that value (Bar Sync or Online); measured on device, a 21 ms guard buffer needed exactly Nudge −21. When Cubasis is the sender, the sign flips: turn Multi-core off there, or use Bar Sync on the receiver with a positive Nudge of the same size. AUM has no such buffer.
  • Recording into Ableton Live (LAN): enable Sync to Incoming Audio under Live's Settings, Link for sample-accurate placement; raise Live's Link-Audio latency slider until playback is stable, and set Monitor to In.

The two hosts' own metronomes are not aligned to each other over the internet. Each side hears the peer on their own grid. In a remote jam that is exactly what you want, since each player only hears their own device.

8 · Troubleshooting

No channel appears in Now Receiving (LAN)

  • Everything on one device? Look for the This device / This host tag: those channels work without Wi-Fi, network permission or Link (§2).
  • Same Wi-Fi network? Some routers isolate clients (“AP/client isolation”). Disable that, or use a hotspot.
  • Open the LinkEffekt app once (Local Network permission). The plugin shows an orange banner if this is missing.
  • On the sender: gear, then Ableton Link and Share Audio must be on.

Channel selected, but no sound: read the diagnostics line

No audio from the sender… · the sender isn't streaming: Share Audio off, Send Audio off, or a different network.
…the host is not running this plugin. · the host never renders the channel. In AUM: give the channel a source, or use Receive Instrument as the source.
Receiving audio, but “Mute Received” / “Mute Output” is ON · un-mute the control it names (also shown when the master gain sits at silence).
Free limit reached… · second Receive instance without Unlimited Channels; unlock or remove the other instance.
No audio from the peer yet… · (Online) the peer left the room or isn't streaming.

Crackles or dropouts

The latency is fixed once you pick a channel, so a connection that is less steady than at that moment produces dropouts rather than a moving number. When more than two occur in ten minutes, the Receive shows their count under the Latency slider: raise the value until it stays quiet. On weak Wi-Fi move closer to the router, or use a direct connection on one device.

The receiver goes quiet for about two seconds, or jumps once

The connection changed under it, e.g. the sender switched Live Mode on or off. The receiver sets its latency again and plays on (§5, Latency).

Feedback howling

The receiving track has a live mic input feeding through. Keep Host track muted (the default) in the Receive mixer, or use the Receive Instrument (no input at all).

I hear the track twice (sender side)

That is the dry signal plus the stream. Keep Mute Local Output on in the Send.

Everything in my sending app sounds a bit late

That is the Send rendering ahead for its receivers (§7). Turn Live Mode on in the Send when you play live there.

Online: “Room not found”

Code typo (codes are letters/numbers only; pasted spaces are removed automatically), or the room expired because it stayed empty. Create a fresh room.

Online: stuck on “waiting for peer”

The other side hasn't joined yet or joined a mistyped code. Re-share the code; both sides need Unlimited Channels.

Beat Sync won't lock (Online)

Is the host transport playing on both sides? Same tempo? The status line names what's missing, and a tempo mismatch is shown with both values.

Timing is stable but consistently early or late (LAN)

On the receiver, turn on Bar Sync and use Nudge under Advanced timing (±100 ms). Without Bar Sync there is nothing to nudge: audio cannot play earlier than the sender delivers it. Sender-side there is also the Link Offset slider in the Send's gear settings.

Received audio sits slightly behind my local tracks

That constant offset is the Latency value plus whatever your host adds after the plugin, and it is by design (§7). If the sender runs in AUM, it plays on the grid anyway. Otherwise, for grid-accurate recording: Bar Sync on, then Nudge to taste; afterwards shift the recorded region by the whole bars Bar Sync added.

9 · Frequently asked questions

Does LinkEffekt work with Ableton Live? Yes. Live 12.4+ receives Link Audio directly (§7), and LinkEffekt Send appears there as an input channel.

Does it work without a network? Yes. Between apps on one device (channels tagged This device / This host) no network, permission or Link setup is needed (§2). Only cross-device LAN and Online need connectivity.

How many channels can I run? With Unlimited Channels: as many Send/Receive instances as your device handles; Online supports up to 8 streams per direction per host.

Does audio go through your servers? No. Online audio is peer-to-peer and DTLS-encrypted; the server only relays the room handshake. If a direct connection is impossible, an encrypted relay (TURN) forwards the still-encrypted stream.

Why is there always some delay? Physics: network transit plus safety buffering. LinkEffekt makes the delay stable and, with Beat Sync, musical (§7). With a sender in AUM it lands right on your grid.

Which sample rates are supported? The host's rate (44.1/48/96 kHz…) on both sides; LinkEffekt converts internally. Online always transmits at 48 kHz/Opus.
